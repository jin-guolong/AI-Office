# Industrial B2B SEO Monthly Report Template

## 1. Purpose

This template defines the standard for monthly client-facing SEO and website operations reports for overseas industrial B2B websites. The report must explain what changed, why it matters to qualified buyers and business performance, what work was completed, and what should happen next.

The report must not be a list of activities or isolated traffic metrics. It must connect Google visibility, landing-page behavior, inquiries, content quality, and implementation work to the client’s commercial objectives.

### 3.2 Client summary

Use three to five concise points:

- `[MOST IMPORTANT BUSINESS OUTCOME]`

- `[BEST-PERFORMING PRODUCT/SOLUTION/PAGE GROUP]`
- `[PRIMARY RISK OR BLOCKER]`
- `[MOST IMPORTANT NEXT ACTION]`

### 3.3 Management interpretation

Write one short paragraph that answers:

- Did qualified visibility and buyer progression improve?
- Which product, solution, or market drove the change?
- What evidence supports the conclusion?
- What decision or resource is required next?

Avoid generic statements such as “SEO performance improved” without magnitude, scope, and business meaning.

Priority definitions:

- **Critical:** crawling, indexation, tracking, conversion, or core-function failure
- **High:** material impact on priority products, solutions, markets, or qualified inquiries
- **Medium:** meaningful supporting improvement after high-priority work
- **Low:** optional refinement, monitoring, or controlled test

Keep the committed plan realistic. Separate planned work from optional recommendations.

## 12. Data Notes and Methodology

Document:

- Metric definitions and conversion/event rules
- Query classification method
- Page-type grouping method
- Filters, exclusions, and sampling/threshold limitations
- Attribution limitations
- Data anomalies and tracking changes
- Forecast assumptions, if any

Never promise rankings, traffic, leads, or revenue. Forecasts must be labeled as scenarios with assumptions.

## 13. Client-Facing Writing Rules

- Lead with decisions and outcomes, not tool names.
- Explain technical issues in business language.
- Use exact values and comparison bases.
- Separate facts, interpretations, and recommendations.
- Avoid blame; identify owners, dependencies, and next actions objectively.
- Avoid visible internal SEO instructions, keyword-stuffing language, or ranking guarantees.
- Use professional English unless the client has requested another language.
- Keep appendices available for detail rather than overcrowding the main narrative.
