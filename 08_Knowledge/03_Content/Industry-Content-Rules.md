# Industry Article Rules for Industrial B2B English Websites

## 1. Purpose

This document defines mandatory production and review standards for English articles about industrial machinery, electrical equipment, and industrial components. Codex must use these rules when planning, drafting, editing, or auditing customer-facing content.

The article must help an overseas industrial buyer understand, evaluate, specify, implement, maintain, or source a real product or solution. It must not be written primarily to repeat keywords or increase publishing volume.

## 2. Audience and Language

Write for a defined reader:

- Engineer or technical specialist
- Procurement or sourcing manager
- Maintenance or operations team
- Project owner, EPC, OEM, or system integrator
- Distributor or channel partner

Language requirements:

- Use professional, clear English
- Select US, UK, or market-specific usage and remain consistent
- Prefer precise technical terms over promotional adjectives
- Define specialized terms when the intended reader may not know them
- Use active voice where it improves clarity
- Keep sentences and paragraphs readable without oversimplifying technical facts
- Use SI units by default and provide market-relevant equivalents when verified and useful
- Preserve official standard, model, material, and certification names

## 3. Source and Fact-Control Rules

Before drafting, create a source list containing:

- Approved product datasheets, drawings, manuals, catalogs, and test records
- Applicable standards and official regulatory guidance
- Approved certification scope
- Engineering notes or subject-matter interviews
- Verified case records and customer approvals
- Current website pages that define product terminology

Evidence hierarchy:

1. Approved internal technical source or official standard/regulator
2. Manufacturer-issued source for the specific product
3. Verified engineering review
4. Credible primary research or industry body
5. Secondary source used only with caution

Codex must never invent:

- Specifications, tolerances, ratings, materials, dimensions, or compatibility
- Standards compliance, certification, patents, testing, or approvals
- Customer names, quotations, cases, performance data, savings, or service life
- Manufacturing capacity, lead time, MOQ, price, warranty, or geographic coverage

Mark missing facts as `[INFORMATION REQUIRED]` in drafts intended for internal review. Remove all internal markers before publication.

## 4. Required Article Brief

Every article requires:

- Working title
- Target market and language variant
- Buyer role and technical level
- Primary search intent and buyer stage
- Core buyer question
- Product/solution/business connection
- Existing canonical page and overlap check
- Required technical sources and reviewer
- Proposed outline
- Evidence, table, diagram, image, or example requirements
- Internal links and CTA
- Publication owner and review status where known

Do not draft when the article’s purpose, source basis, and intended buyer action are undefined.

## 5. Article Structure

Use the smallest structure that completely answers the buyer need.

### 5.1 Opening

- State the problem, task, or decision directly
- Identify the relevant product/application context
- Explain what the reader will be able to decide or do
- Avoid generic industry introductions and inflated market claims

### 5.2 Definitions and operating context

- Define necessary concepts
- State assumptions, units, environment, and system boundaries
- Distinguish similar product types or terms

### 5.3 Main technical explanation

- Organize around buyer questions or decision steps
- Explain cause-and-effect relationships
- Use tables for comparisons and structured data
- Use diagrams where relationships or workflows are difficult to explain in prose
- Connect features to performance, reliability, maintenance, safety, sourcing, or lifecycle value

### 5.4 Selection, application, or procedure

- Provide criteria in a logical order
- State required inputs and decision limits
- Explain trade-offs and incompatible conditions
- Identify when supplier or qualified engineering review is required

### 5.5 Evidence and limitations

- Reference applicable standards, tests, specifications, or verified cases
- State conditions behind quantitative claims
- Explain limitations and project-specific variables

### 5.6 Next step

- Summarize the decision, not the entire article
- Link to relevant products, solutions, resources, or assistance
- Use a stage-appropriate CTA

## 6. Industry-Specific Requirements

### 6.1 Industrial machinery

Address as applicable:

- Process, material, output, accuracy, cycle, capacity, and automation needs
- Machine configuration and upstream/downstream integration
- Utilities, footprint, environment, installation, and commissioning
- Changeover, cleaning, maintenance, wear parts, and service access
- Guarding, safety procedures, operator requirements, and applicable standards
- Throughput or performance assumptions and test conditions

Safety-critical instructions require approved technical review. Do not advise bypassing interlocks, guards, lockout/tagout, or manufacturer procedures.

### 6.2 Electrical equipment

Address as applicable:

- Voltage, current, frequency, phase, power, fault level, duty, and load profile
- Protection, coordination, selectivity, derating, temperature, altitude, and enclosure
- IP/NEMA classification, insulation, creepage/clearance, EMC, and communication interfaces
- IEC, UL, NEC, regional code, and certification scope
- Wiring, installation, testing, inspection, and maintenance boundaries
- System-level compatibility rather than component specifications alone

Do not provide unsafe live-work instructions. State when work requires a qualified electrical professional and local-code verification.

### 6.3 Industrial components

Address as applicable:

- Material, grade, dimensions, tolerances, surface treatment, and manufacturing process
- Load, speed, pressure, temperature, medium, contamination, and environment
- Interface, fit, interchangeability, mounting, sealing, lubrication, and compatibility
- Wear, corrosion, fatigue, vibration, leakage, and expected failure modes
- Inspection, traceability, testing, packaging, storage, and replacement
- Drawing revision and sample/first-article validation for custom parts

Do not claim drop-in replacement or compatibility without verified dimensional, material, interface, and performance data.

## 7. Comparison and Calculation Rules

- Compare products using equivalent conditions and criteria
- Cite the source or internal approved basis for numeric values
- Show units, variables, assumptions, and rounding
- Distinguish rated, tested, calculated, nominal, typical, and maximum values
- Do not generalize one test result to every configuration
- Explain trade-offs; do not force a universal winner
- Have safety-, sizing-, load-, thermal-, pressure-, or electrical-critical calculations reviewed

## 8. Claims and Commercial Language

Allowed claims must be specific, supportable, and scoped.

Replace vague language:

- “High quality” → identify inspection, material, tolerance, test, or quality-control evidence
- “Reliable” → state applicable design, test, duty, or maintenance evidence
- “Cost-effective” → explain which cost drivers are affected and under what conditions
- “Easy to maintain” → describe access, interval, parts, or procedure evidence

Prohibited without approved evidence:

- Best, No. 1, leading, guaranteed, zero maintenance, failure-proof, universally compatible
- Guaranteed savings, life, efficiency, safety, compliance, delivery, or ROI
- Competitor criticism that is unverified or unfair

## 9. Search and Page-Quality Rules

- Assign one primary intent and preferred canonical URL
- Write a unique, accurate title and H1
- Answer the main question early
- Use headings based on the decision process, not keyword variations
- Cover necessary related concepts naturally
- Avoid hidden SEO text, keyword lists, repetitive exact-match phrasing, and copied summaries
- Add descriptive internal links to the relevant pillar, commercial page, and supporting resources
- Keep visible content consistent with metadata and structured data
- Update or consolidate existing content instead of publishing near-duplicates

## 10. CTA Rules

Match the CTA to the article stage:

- Discovery: explore product range or application overview
- Evaluation: compare options, use a selection checklist, or view solution
- Validation: download technical data, review evidence, or discuss requirements
- Specification: request selection support or submit specifications/drawings
- Implementation/maintenance: consult documentation or technical support
- Sourcing: request a quote or supplier review

Do not interrupt safety or technical instructions with aggressive sales language.

## 11. Editorial and Technical Review Workflow

1. Validate brief, intent, page boundary, and business relevance.
2. Collect approved sources and record missing information.
3. Draft using the required article structure.
4. Verify every specification, standard, number, claim, and compatibility statement.
5. Obtain subject-matter review for technical and safety-sensitive content.
6. Edit for professional English, clarity, consistency, and buyer usefulness.
7. Check metadata, headings, internal links, media, CTA, and accessibility.
8. Remove internal notes and confirm publication approval.
9. Review rendered desktop/mobile output after implementation.
10. Monitor queries, engagement, assisted actions, and update needs.

## 12. Codex Checklist

- [ ] Target market, reader, intent, and buyer stage are defined
- [ ] Article supports a real product, solution, application, or buyer task
- [ ] Approved technical sources and reviewer are identified
- [ ] Opening answers the core question without generic filler
- [ ] Technical terms, units, specifications, and standards are accurate
- [ ] Machinery, electrical, or component-specific factors are addressed
- [ ] Assumptions, trade-offs, limitations, and review boundaries are stated
- [ ] Claims, calculations, comparisons, and cases are supported
- [ ] No specification, compatibility, certification, or result is invented
- [ ] Safety-critical content has qualified review
- [ ] Article is distinct from existing pages and avoids cannibalization
- [ ] Internal links and CTA support the buyer’s next decision
- [ ] English style and terminology are consistent
- [ ] Metadata, media, links, and rendered layout are checked
- [ ] Internal placeholders and review notes are removed before publication
