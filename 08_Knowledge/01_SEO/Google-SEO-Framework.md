# Google SEO Framework for Industrial B2B Websites

> Codex internal execution rules. Use this file when analyzing an overseas industrial website, defining its SEO strategy, mapping keywords, evaluating pages, or producing an optimization plan.

## Scope

This framework applies to English-language independent websites for:

- Industrial machinery and production equipment
- Electrical equipment, power distribution, protection, and control products
- Industrial components, replacement parts, and custom/OEM components
- Related systems, applications, technical resources, and project solutions

Codex must use buyer-oriented, evidence-based analysis. Do not promise rankings, traffic, inquiries, or revenue. Do not invent product specifications, standards, certifications, applications, compatibility, customer cases, capacity, performance, pricing, or delivery claims.

# 1. SEO Objectives and Principles

## 1.1 Core objectives

Industrial B2B SEO must serve three business outcomes.

### Acquire qualified procurement traffic

SEO must attract buyers whose searches match the company’s real products, technical capabilities, applications, and target markets.

Qualified traffic includes:

- Engineers validating products, specifications, standards, or compatibility
- Procurement teams comparing products, manufacturers, supply capability, and documentation
- Project owners and integrators evaluating solutions, systems, and implementation support
- OEMs searching for custom components, drawings, samples, testing, and manufacturing capability
- Distributors reviewing product range, supply scope, certification, and market support

Codex must not treat high-volume but commercially irrelevant visits as success.

### Establish topical authority in the real industrial field

The website must provide connected, accurate coverage around the company’s actual products and expertise:

- Product families and models
- Materials, ratings, technologies, and standards
- Applications, industries, and operating environments
- Solution approaches and product combinations
- Selection, configuration, integration, installation, and maintenance knowledge
- Manufacturing, testing, quality, documentation, and case evidence

Topic coverage must remain focused. Do not publish unrelated trending content or broad industrial articles with no connection to the company’s offer.

### Support inquiry conversion

SEO traffic must progress to a useful buyer action:

- Product/category exploration
- Solution or application evaluation
- Datasheet, drawing, manual, or catalog download
- Product selection or engineering consultation
- RFQ, sample, OEM, distributor, call, or email inquiry

Every priority page must have a stage-appropriate next step and measurable conversion path.

## 1.2 Operating principles

Codex must follow these rules:

1. Business relevance before search volume.
2. Buyer intent before keyword placement.
3. One preferred canonical page for each primary intent.
4. Verified technical value before promotional language.
5. Page completeness before metadata rewriting.
6. Commercial hubs before large-scale Blog production.
7. Internal links must support hierarchy and buyer progression.
8. First-party GSC, GA4, CRM, sales, and support evidence takes priority where available.
9. Observations, inferences, and unvalidated hypotheses must be labeled separately.
10. URL, redirect, canonical, indexation, tracking, or deployment changes require impact review and approval.

## 1.3 Measurement hierarchy

| Layer | Required measures |
|---|---|
| Technical eligibility | Intended crawlability, rendering, indexation, canonical state, tracking quality |
| Search visibility | Priority non-brand queries, target-country impressions/clicks, page-type visibility |
| Buyer engagement | Product/Solution progression, downloads, CTA use, form starts |
| Conversion | RFQs, calls, emails, samples, consultations, distributor/OEM inquiries |
| Business quality | Qualified inquiry rate, target-product/market fit, opportunity value where CRM data exists |

Codex must not equate impressions with visits, clicks with sessions, users with leads, or submissions with qualified opportunities.

## 1.4 Required strategy output

Every SEO strategy must identify:

- Target markets, buyers, products, solutions, and conversions
- Data sources, date ranges, filters, and limitations
- Existing page inventory and preferred page roles
- Keyword-to-page map
- Topic and page gaps
- Critical, High, Medium, and Low actions
- Dependencies, risks, owners, and approval requirements
- Baseline, validation method, and review period

# 2. Industrial B2B Search Characteristics

## 2.1 Long procurement cycle

Industrial purchasing commonly moves through several stages:

`Problem recognition → Product/technology discovery → Comparison → Technical validation → Supplier validation → Specification → RFQ → Project review`

### Codex rules

- Do not evaluate all keywords as immediate purchase intent.
- Build page paths for Awareness, Consideration, and Decision stages.
- Keep early-stage guides connected to Category, Product, Solution, or Application pages.
- Provide technical evidence before asking for high-commitment inquiry details.
- Track assisted buyer progression, not only last-click form submissions.
- Maintain useful content for long review periods; update data and documents when products or standards change.

## 2.2 Technical search behavior

Industrial buyers commonly search using combinations of:

- Product type or model
- Material, size, tolerance, capacity, rating, voltage, current, power, load, speed, pressure, temperature, or medium
- Standard, certification, protection class, interface, mounting, connection, or communication protocol
- Application, process, machine, environment, failure, maintenance, or replacement need
- Manufacturer, supplier, custom, OEM, datasheet, drawing, manual, quote, or distributor modifier

### Codex rules

- Preserve model numbers, standards, units, and technical terminology accurately.
- Distinguish rated, nominal, typical, tested, calculated, minimum, and maximum values.
- Do not map an exact specification query to a generic homepage.
- Do not write technical content without approved sources and appropriate review.
- Flag machinery safety, electrical safety, pressure, thermal, load, chemical, hazardous-area, and regulatory topics for qualified review.
- Never confirm compatibility or compliance from a product name alone.

## 2.3 Multiple decision roles

One industrial purchase may involve several roles with different questions.

| Role | Primary questions | Required website evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Engineer | Will it fit, perform, integrate, and comply? | Specifications, drawings, principles, standards, limits, technical support |
| Procurement | Is the supplier credible and commercially suitable? | Manufacturer role, quality, capacity, documentation, supply/support process |
| Project owner/EPC | Can the system meet project requirements? | Solution architecture, responsibilities, cases, testing, commissioning |
| Maintenance/operations | Can it be installed, operated, serviced, and replaced? | Manuals, access, spare parts, troubleshooting, lifecycle support |
| OEM/integrator | Can it be customized and integrated? | Interfaces, drawings, engineering, samples, testing, change control |
| Distributor | Is the range, supply, certification, and support suitable? | Portfolio, market support, documentation, partnership contact |

### Codex rules

- Identify the primary and secondary reader for every page.
- Do not make one page answer every role when dedicated pages are clearer.
- Ensure technical and commercial information does not contradict.
- Use CTAs appropriate to the reader: compare, download, submit requirements, discuss application, or request quote.

## 2.4 Product plus application searches

Industrial intent often combines an entity with operating context:

- `[product] for [industry/process]`
- `[equipment] for [material/output]`
- `[component] for [load/environment/medium]`
- `[electrical product] for [system/rating/application]`
- `[machine] for [product format/capacity]`

### Codex rules

- Product pages explain exact product fit and limitations.
- Category pages explain range-level selection.
- Application pages explain where/how products are used.
- Solution pages explain how a problem or system requirement is addressed.
- Do not create an Application page for every keyword combination.
- Create a dedicated page only when the application has distinct requirements, products, evidence, and buyer value.
- Product-to-application claims must be technically verified and linked in both directions where useful.

## 2.5 Industry-specific analysis dimensions

### Industrial machinery

Check process, material, capacity, output, accuracy, automation, utilities, footprint, integration, safety, commissioning, changeover, cleaning, maintenance, and spare parts.

### Electrical equipment

Check voltage, current, power, frequency, phase, duty, fault level, protection, coordination, derating, enclosure, environment, communication, installation, testing, and applicable standards.

### Industrial components

Check material, grade, dimensions, tolerance, load, speed, pressure, temperature, medium, fit, interface, sealing, lubrication, corrosion, wear, fatigue, traceability, and replacement compatibility.

# 3. Website Topic Architecture

## 3.1 Core relationship

Use this as the buyer and topic flow:

```text
Homepage
   ↓
Category
   ↓
Product
   ↓
Solution
   ↓
Application
   ↓
Blog / Knowledge
```

This diagram defines connected page roles; it does not require every URL to be nested in this exact order. The actual architecture must use clear hubs, crawlable links, breadcrumbs, and reciprocal contextual links.

## 3.2 Page-role rules

### Homepage

Owns company/brand and broad commercial positioning. Routes buyers to priority Categories, Solutions, Applications, capabilities, proof, knowledge, and inquiry.

Must not target every product model or reproduce every technical detail.

### Category

Owns broad product-family intent. Explains product types, classification, selection criteria, filters, comparison, applications, and links to Product pages.

Must not become a thin product grid or compete with exact model pages.

### Product

Owns exact product/model intent. Provides specifications, options, working principle, applications, materials, evidence, documents, and product-specific RFQ.

Must link to its Category, verified Solutions/Applications, evidence, related resources, and inquiry.

### Solution

Owns problem, system, process, or integrated project intent. Connects pain points, requirements, technical approach, products, project process, evidence, and consultation.

Must not be a renamed Category or unexplained product list.

### Application

Owns industry, machine, environment, medium, or use-condition context. Explains requirements, fit, limitations, relevant Products, and related Solutions.

Must not consist of duplicated text where only the industry name changes.

### Blog/Knowledge

Owns narrow education, comparison, technical validation, specification, installation, maintenance, troubleshooting, or sourcing tasks. Supports the commercial hierarchy.

Must not replace the correct Category, Product, Solution, or Application page for strong commercial intent.

## 3.3 Required link relationships

| Source | Must normally link to |
|---|---|
| Homepage | Priority Categories, Solutions, Applications, capability/quality, cases, knowledge, contact |
| Category | Child Products, selection guides, Applications, Solutions, FAQ, inquiry |
| Product | Parent Category, relevant Solutions/Applications, cases, documents, related products, RFQ |
| Solution | Solution hub, Products, Applications, cases, guides, project inquiry |
| Application | Relevant Products, Categories, Solutions, cases, technical resources, consultation |
| Blog/Knowledge | Pillar/hub, relevant commercial page, supporting content, appropriate CTA |

Every priority indexable page must have a crawlable internal inlink and an onward buyer path. XML sitemaps do not replace internal links.

## 3.4 Pillar and supporting model

- Product Category or Solution/Application hub normally acts as the commercial Pillar Page.
- Product, Application, Case, FAQ, Guide, and resource pages support a defined pillar.
- Supporting pages must link back to the pillar and to the relevant commercial next step.
- One preferred canonical page must own each primary intent.
- Orphans, dead ends, uncontrolled filters, parameter duplicates, and competing hubs must be flagged.

## 3.5 Architecture acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every page type has a distinct business and search role
- [ ] Priority pages are reachable through logical crawlable links
- [ ] Categories and Products use an approved taxonomy
- [ ] Solutions and Applications are distinguished by buyer task
- [ ] Blog/Knowledge supports, rather than competes with, commercial pages
- [ ] Breadcrumbs, navigation, internal anchors, canonicals, and sitemaps are consistent
- [ ] Buyer paths reach a measurable conversion action

# 4. Keyword Mapping Rules

## 4.1 Mandatory mapping fields

Every keyword cluster must include:

| Field | Required value |
|---|---|
| Representative queries | Verified variants, models, standards, and modifiers |
| Keyword class | Core, Product, Application, Technical, or Industry |
| Buyer role | Engineer, procurement, owner, OEM, integrator, distributor, maintenance |
| Buyer stage | Awareness, Consideration, or Decision |
| Primary intent | One buyer task |
| Secondary intent | Optional |
| Preferred page type | Homepage, Category, Product, Solution, Application, Blog/FAQ/Resource |
| Preferred URL | One canonical target |
| Required coverage | Questions, data, proof, and sections |
| Internal links | Parent, child, supporting, evidence, CTA |
| Evidence | GSC, SERP, product taxonomy, sales/support, CRM |
| Confidence | High, Medium, or Low |

## 4.2 Core keywords

### Definition

Broad terms representing the company’s primary commercial field, core product families, or major solution domain.

Examples:

- `industrial packaging machinery`
- `low voltage electrical equipment`
- `custom industrial components`

### Mapping rules

- Homepage may target the company-level core offer.
- Category/Solution hubs target distinct major commercial themes.
- Do not assign all core keywords to the homepage.
- Do not create overlapping hubs for close synonyms.
- A core term must reflect the company’s real offer and target buyer.

## 4.3 Product keywords

### Definition

Product family, subtype, model, material, rating, configuration, supplier, manufacturer, custom, quote, datasheet, or drawing searches.

### Mapping rules

- Broad family → Category
- Defined subtype → Subcategory or Category section
- Exact model/product → Product Detail
- Multi-product supplier/manufacturer intent → Category or capability-supported commercial page
- Exact data/document intent → Product Detail plus appropriate resource
- Do not map a specific model query to Homepage.
- Use “manufacturer,” material, rating, standard, and compatibility only when verified.

## 4.4 Application keywords

### Definition

Queries combining a Product/Equipment with industry, process, machine, environment, material, medium, or use condition.

### Mapping rules

- Product-specific verified use → Product page section
- Distinct operating context → Application page
- Multi-product/system need → Solution page
- Range selection for one use → Category/Application page
- Educational “how/why/select” need → Blog/Guide linked to the commercial page
- Create a separate Application page only when requirements and content are materially unique.

## 4.5 Technical keywords

### Definition

Specifications, standards, calculations, comparison, compatibility, installation, testing, maintenance, troubleshooting, and engineering explanations.

### Mapping rules

- Exact model specifications → Product Detail
- Category selection criteria → Category or selection guide
- System design/problem solving → Solution or technical guide
- Narrow recurring question → FAQ
- Manual/drawing/certificate intent → HTML context plus approved download
- High-risk technical content requires qualified review.
- Do not answer unsafe or project-specific questions without conditions and professional boundaries.

## 4.6 Industry keywords

### Definition

Queries naming an industry or industrial segment, often combined with Products, Applications, or Solutions.

Examples:

- `packaging machinery for food manufacturing`
- `electrical protection for chemical plants`
- `components for renewable energy equipment`

### Mapping rules

- Industry + product-range intent → Category/Application
- Industry + system/problem intent → Solution
- Industry + technical question → Guide/Blog
- Industry page must contain specific processes, requirements, risks, products, evidence, and CTA.
- Do not generate multiple industry pages from one template with only the industry name changed.

## 4.7 Cannibalization control

Flag pages when they share the same primary intent, similar Titles/H1s, overlapping GSC query sets, competing internal anchors, or substantially duplicated answers.

Resolution workflow:

1. Confirm whether buyer tasks are actually the same.
2. Select the preferred page using business role, content, links, conversions, backlinks, and canonical suitability.
3. Differentiate, consolidate, redirect, canonicalize, noindex, or retire only after impact review.
4. Obtain approval for URL, redirect, canonical, indexation, or content-removal changes.
5. Update internal links, sitemap, schema, metadata, and tracking consistently.

# 5. Page SEO Evaluation Standard

## 5.1 Evaluation method

Score each dimension:

- **0 — Fail:** missing, incorrect, unsupported, misleading, or technically blocked
- **1 — Partial:** present but incomplete, generic, weakly supported, or difficult to use
- **2 — Pass:** complete for the page purpose, specific, verified, accessible, and actionable

A Critical technical or factual failure cannot be offset by a high total score.

## 5.2 Search Intent

### Pass standard

- Buyer, stage, primary task, and page type are defined.
- Title, H1, opening, main sections, internal links, and CTA serve the same intent.
- One preferred canonical page owns the intent.
- SERP result type and GSC query/page behavior have been reviewed where available.
- Page does not compete unnecessarily with another internal URL.

### Fail indicators

- Product query leads to generic Homepage.
- Solution query leads to unexplained product grid.
- Technical comparison leads to biased sales copy.
- Informational Blog targets the same primary commercial intent as Category/Product.

## 5.3 Content Depth

### Pass standard

The page includes the facts and decision support required for its type:

- Category: range, classification, selection, applications, comparison, proof
- Product: specifications, options, principles, applications, materials, evidence, documents, RFQ
- Solution: pain points, requirements, technical approach, products, process, proof, consultation
- Application: operating context, requirements, fit, limits, products, solutions, cases
- Blog/Guide: direct answer, variables, method, evidence, limitations, next step

Depth is determined by buyer need, not word count.

### Fail indicators

- Generic marketing copy replaces technical information.
- Essential information is available only in a PDF.
- Claims lack source, scope, condition, or evidence.
- Buyer must search another site for basic selection information.

## 5.4 Topical Coverage

### Pass standard

- The page covers necessary entities, attributes, applications, standards, and buyer questions naturally.
- Supporting pages cover narrower tasks without duplicating the pillar.
- Product, Solution, Application, Case, FAQ, Blog, and resource relationships are connected.
- The cluster covers relevant Awareness, Consideration, and Decision needs.
- Coverage remains within the company’s real expertise.

### Fail indicators

- Many keyword pages repeat the same content.
- Important technical or application subtopics have no supporting content.
- Blog volume is high but commercial hubs are incomplete.
- Related content has no internal-link or conversion relationship.

## 5.5 User Experience

### Pass standard

- Clear module order follows buyer decision logic.
- Navigation, breadcrumbs, internal links, filters, comparison, downloads, and forms work.
- Mobile and desktop provide equivalent primary content and actions.
- Tables, diagrams, images, and media are readable, accurate, accessible, and performant.
- Main content and links exist in rendered HTML.
- Page has valid metadata, canonical, intended robots state, and applicable structured data.
- Tracking, consent, verification, and integrations remain functional.

### Fail indicators

- Mobile users cannot read specifications or use forms.
- Important links use noncrawlable script actions.
- Slow/unstable media obscures primary content.
- Filters generate uncontrolled duplicate URLs.
- Broken, redirected, blocked, noindex, or staging links appear in priority paths.

## 5.6 Conversion Path

### Pass standard

- CTA matches Awareness, Consideration, or Decision stage.
- Product, Solution, Application, and source context persists through the inquiry.
- Forms ask only for information required at that stage.
- Complex products allow drawing/specification upload where appropriate and secure.
- CTA clicks, downloads, form starts, errors, submissions, calls, and emails are measurable.
- Buyers can choose direct RFQ, assisted selection, technical validation, or project consultation as appropriate.

### Fail indicators

- Only generic “Contact Us” links exist.
- RFQ loses product/model or Solution context.
- Early-stage pages demand excessive personal data.
- Page promises instant price or engineering approval without review.
- No measurable next action exists.

## 5.7 Priority rules

- **Critical:** blocks crawling, indexation, tracking, conversion, security, core function, or factual integrity
- **High:** affects priority products, Solutions, markets, buyer intent, or qualified inquiries
- **Medium:** improves supporting coverage, linking, usability, or evidence
- **Low:** optional refinement, monitoring, or controlled test

# 6. Google Quality Signals

## Operating interpretation

Codex must use Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trust as a practical quality and risk framework. E-E-A-T is not a standalone score or a single ranking factor. Trust is the controlling requirement.

## 6.1 Experience

Demonstrate real first-hand industrial experience through approved evidence:

- Application conditions and operating constraints
- Engineering, selection, integration, installation, commissioning, maintenance, or troubleshooting knowledge
- Real product, factory, test, process, and project media
- Case studies showing challenge, configuration, implementation, conditions, and results
- Trade-offs, failure modes, limitations, and lessons learned
- Samples, prototypes, drawings, inspection, and production records

### Codex checks

- [ ] Experience is specific to the product/application and not simulated
- [ ] Media and cases are real, approved, and accurately labeled
- [ ] Operating conditions and limitations are disclosed
- [ ] AI-generated copy does not invent first-hand experience

## 6.2 Expertise

Demonstrate technical competence through:

- Qualified authors or technical reviewers where appropriate
- Correct product terminology, units, standards, calculations, and document revisions
- Accurate product principles, selection criteria, compatibility, and safety boundaries
- Engineering, manufacturing, testing, quality, and documentation methods
- Clear distinction between general guidance and project-specific approval

### Codex checks

- [ ] Author/reviewer background matches the topic
- [ ] Technical sources and revisions are recorded internally
- [ ] Specifications, standards, compatibility, and claims are verified
- [ ] High-risk machinery/electrical/pressure/chemical content has qualified review
- [ ] Missing facts are flagged rather than inferred

## 6.3 Authoritativeness

Build authority through focused, verifiable coverage and recognized relationships:

- Complete product and solution clusters in the company’s real field
- Original technical resources, data, testing, diagrams, cases, and documentation
- Consistent company, brand, product, author, and organization identity
- Relevant certifications, patents, standards work, industry memberships, distributors, customers, publications, and partnerships when approved
- Useful resources that earn legitimate references and links

### Codex checks

- [ ] Website focus matches the company’s real products and expertise
- [ ] Topic clusters are complete and internally connected
- [ ] Claims of recognition, membership, partnership, or market position are approved
- [ ] External authority signals are earned and relevant, not purchased or fabricated
- [ ] Competitor content is not copied or lightly rewritten

## 6.4 Trust

Trust requires accuracy, transparency, safety, and accountability:

- Clear company identity, business role, address/contact, and ownership information
- Privacy, cookie, terms, warranty, support, and commercial policies where applicable
- Accurate certification scope, standards, product status, and document revisions
- Clear source basis for technical, quantitative, and performance claims
- Approved customer names, logos, quotations, images, and case results
- Transparent dates and meaningful updates; no fake freshness
- Secure forms/uploads, consent, and responsible handling of personal/project data
- Correction of errors and outdated information

### Codex checks

- [ ] Company role and contact information are clear and consistent
- [ ] Company certification is distinguished from product certification
- [ ] Claims include source, scope, unit, condition, and limitation where needed
- [ ] Measured, calculated, customer-reported, and supplier-observed results are distinguished
- [ ] No fabricated reviews, ratings, prices, stock, customers, results, or structured data exist
- [ ] Forms, consent, security, tracking, and privacy behavior are functional
- [ ] AI assistance receives human source verification and technical review

## 6.5 E-E-A-T implementation by page type

| Page type | Required quality evidence |
|---|---|
| Homepage | Company identity, manufacturing role, capability, quality, certifications, cases, contact |
| Category | Product taxonomy, selection logic, verified range data, applications, quality evidence |
| Product | Specifications, documents, materials, principles, tests, certification scope, support |
| Solution | Industry requirements, engineering approach, products, implementation, cases, limitations |
| Application | Operating context, selection conditions, verified product fit, standards, cases |
| Blog/Guide | Author/reviewer, technical sources, original analysis, methods, limitations, update status |
| Case Study | Customer approval, configuration, process evidence, result method, conditions, testimonial source |

## 6.6 Final framework checklist

- [ ] SEO objectives connect qualified traffic, topical authority, and inquiry conversion
- [ ] Industrial buyer cycle, technical search, decision roles, and product+application behavior are addressed
- [ ] Homepage, Category, Product, Solution, Application, and Blog roles are distinct and linked
- [ ] Core, Product, Application, Technical, and Industry keywords map to preferred pages
- [ ] Pages pass Search Intent, Content Depth, Topical Coverage, UX, and Conversion checks
- [ ] Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trust are supported by real evidence
- [ ] Critical factual, technical, indexing, tracking, form, or security issues are prioritized first
- [ ] Recommendations include evidence, affected URL/file, priority, dependency, validation, and KPI
- [ ] URL, redirect, canonical, indexation, tracking, and deployment changes have approval
