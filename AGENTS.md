# Page Analysis Workflow

For any website page analysis, follow this sequence:

1. Identify the page type, target market, product or solution, and intended conversion goal.
2. Determine the primary buyer personas, their purchasing stage, pain points, technical concerns, and decision criteria.
3. Inspect the current page structure, navigation, headings, content blocks, media, calls to action, forms, links, metadata, schema, and tracking code.
4. Evaluate the page against search intent, buyer journey, information completeness, usability, internal linking, conversion path, and technical implementation.
5. Check whether the page clearly communicates product fit, technical value, applications, specifications, proof, support, and next steps.
6. Separate findings into critical issues, high-impact opportunities, and optional improvements.
7. Recommend a revised information hierarchy and content outline before proposing detailed copy or code changes.
8. Define expected business impact and suitable measurement indicators.

Every page analysis should cover, when applicable:

- Page objective and primary conversion action
- Target buyer and search intent
- Strengths and gaps in the current structure
- Missing technical or commercial information
- Heading and content hierarchy
- Trust signals and evidence
- Internal links and next-step paths
- CTA clarity and form friction
- Mobile and accessibility concerns
- Technical SEO and implementation risks
- Prioritized recommendations

# SEO Analysis Workflow

For Google SEO analysis, follow this sequence:

1. Confirm the business objective, target countries, languages, product scope, and analysis period.
2. Review available first-party data from GA4, GSC, website crawls, rankings, conversions, and existing content inventories.
3. Map keywords and queries by search intent, buyer stage, topic, page type, and commercial relevance.
4. Check keyword-to-page alignment and identify cannibalization, content gaps, weak landing pages, and unsupported topic clusters.
5. Evaluate technical SEO, including crawlability, indexability, canonicals, robots directives, XML sitemaps, status codes, redirects, duplicate content, rendering, mobile usability, page speed, and structured data.
6. Evaluate on-page elements, including title, meta description, H1-H6 structure, topical coverage, image attributes, anchor text, internal links, and conversion relevance.
7. Compare relevant competitors by page structure, content depth, topic coverage, proof, and SERP positioning without copying their content.
8. Prioritize actions by business impact, evidence strength, effort, dependency, and implementation risk.
9. Establish baseline metrics and define how results will be monitored in GA4 and GSC.

SEO analysis outputs must:

- State the data source and date range
- Distinguish observations from hypotheses
- Avoid promising rankings or traffic outcomes
- Connect SEO opportunities to qualified traffic, inquiries, and business value
- Include prioritized actions, responsible files or page types, and measurement methods

# Content Production Standards

All customer-facing content must:

- Use professional English
- Target industrial buyers
- Explain technical value clearly
- Avoid exaggerated claims
- Match a defined search intent and buyer journey stage
- Use accurate terminology consistently
- Lead with buyer problems, application fit, and decision value
- Support claims with provided specifications, standards, certifications, tests, cases, or other verifiable evidence
- Include a clear and appropriate next step
- Be original, useful, and specific to the product, solution, application, or industry

Before writing content:

1. Confirm the page type, audience, objective, primary topic, target market, and CTA.
2. Review available product data, technical documents, brand terminology, existing pages, and approved claims.
3. Identify missing facts. Mark them as information required instead of inventing details.
4. Create an outline that follows buyer questions and decision logic.

For product detail pages, normally consider:

- Product definition and primary value
- Suitable applications and operating conditions
- Key features linked to buyer benefits
- Technical specifications and selectable options
- Materials, standards, compatibility, and performance evidence
- Installation, operation, maintenance, or customization information
- Quality assurance, certifications, downloads, FAQs, and related products
- Inquiry or quotation CTA

For solution pages, normally consider:

- Industry or operational challenge
- Buyer requirements and constraints
- Proposed solution architecture or workflow
- Relevant products and how they work together
- Technical and commercial value
- Application process, proof, cases, and FAQs
- Consultation or project inquiry CTA

For blog topic clusters:

- Define one pillar topic and supporting cluster topics
- Assign one primary intent and one target page to each topic
- Cover awareness, evaluation, selection, implementation, and troubleshooting stages where relevant
- Define internal links between cluster articles, pillar pages, product pages, and solution pages
- Avoid duplicate topics and keyword cannibalization
- Prioritize topics by business relevance, buyer demand, evidence, and content feasibility

Do not write visible content containing:

- SEO terms
- Ranking statements
- Internal optimization explanations
- Unsupported superlatives such as “best,” “No. 1,” or “industry-leading”
- Fabricated numbers, clients, certifications, standards, or case results

# HTML Modification Rules

Before modifying any HTML:

1. Analyze the existing structure, content, styling, scripts, forms, links, metadata, schema, tracking, and dependencies.
2. Explain the problems, proposed changes, expected value, and implementation risks.
3. Create a dated backup in the same project or an approved backup directory.
4. Generate an optimized version while preserving the original unless replacement is explicitly approved.
5. Validate the resulting HTML, critical links, responsive behavior, visible content, forms, scripts, metadata, schema, and tracking.
6. Report the files changed and verification performed.

When restructuring HTML:

- Prefer semantic HTML and a logical H1-H6 hierarchy
- Preserve valid canonical tags, hreflang, structured data, analytics, pixels, consent tools, and verification tags
- Preserve existing URLs, anchors, form destinations, query parameters, and functional integrations unless approval is given
- Maintain or improve accessibility, keyboard usability, labels, alternative text, and color contrast
- Avoid unnecessary libraries, render-blocking assets, duplicate IDs, inline duplication, and layout shifts
- Keep customer-facing copy professional and free from internal SEO language
- Add comments only when they help future maintenance and do not expose internal strategy publicly

Never:

- Delete tracking codes
- Change URL without approval
- Remove important content
- Replace verified technical facts with assumptions
- Break forms, analytics events, schema, navigation, or internal links
- Overwrite the only copy of a source file
- Publish or deploy changes without explicit authorization

# GA4 and GSC Data Analysis Rules

When analyzing GA4 or GSC data:

1. State the property, view or data source, filters, comparison period, timezone, and known data limitations.
2. Check data quality before interpreting performance.
3. Segment findings by country, device, channel, query, landing page, page type, and conversion where useful.
4. Distinguish correlation from causation and flag insufficient sample sizes.
5. Connect visibility and traffic metrics to engagement, inquiries, conversions, and page quality.
6. Provide findings, likely causes, recommended actions, priority, and measurement plans.

Use precise metric names. Do not treat clicks as sessions, users as leads, or average position as a direct ranking guarantee.

Monthly reports should normally include:

1. Executive summary
2. Business objectives and KPI status
3. GA4 performance and conversions
4. GSC search performance
5. Landing page and content performance
6. SEO and website work completed
7. Key findings and business implications
8. Risks, blockers, and data limitations

Each slide should communicate one primary message, use accurate chart labels, show the reporting period and comparison basis, and translate data into decisions. Avoid decorative charts that do not support a conclusion.

# Output Format Requirements

Whenever creating or modifying files, provide:

1. File name
2. File location
3. Deliverable purpose
4. Summary of changes
5. Data sources or source files used
6. Validation or checks completed
7. Important assumptions, limitations, or unresolved inputs
8. Recommended next action, when applicable

For analysis and recommendations, use this default structure:

1. Objective and scope
2. Executive summary
3. Evidence and key findings
4. Prioritized recommendations
5. Implementation notes
6. Measurement plan
7. Risks, dependencies, and missing information

Priority labels:

- Critical: blocks crawling, conversion, data accuracy, or core functionality
- High: strong expected business impact and should be addressed soon
- Medium: meaningful improvement after higher-priority work
- Low: optional refinement or test opportunity

All final responses must be concise, decision-oriented, and explicit about what was completed. Customer-facing copy should be in professional English; operational explanations and reports may use the user's requested language.
