# Industrial B2B Solution Page Standard

## 1. Page Objective

A solution page must demonstrate how the company addresses a defined industrial problem, application, process, or industry requirement. It must connect buyer challenges to an explainable solution approach, relevant products, technical evidence, implementation support, and project inquiry.

A solution page is not a product list with a new heading. It should help the buyer understand how components, engineering, services, and operating considerations work together.

Primary success signals include product/technical-resource engagement, project consultation actions, qualified solution inquiries, and assisted buyer progression.

## 2. Buyer Decision Path

Recommended path:

`Recognize challenge → Confirm requirements → Understand solution approach → Evaluate products and evidence → Review implementation → Discuss project`

Buyer questions:

1. Does the supplier understand my process, environment, and constraints?
2. What approach is proposed and why?
3. Which products or capabilities are involved?
4. What performance, compatibility, compliance, and integration factors matter?
5. What proof and project support are available?
6. What information is needed to evaluate my project?

## 3. Required Modules

### 3.1 Solution header

- Specific solution/application H1
- Target industry, process, or operational problem
- Concise value proposition with no unsupported outcome claims
- Primary consultation CTA

### 3.2 Buyer challenge and context

- Operating environment and process context
- Common risks, inefficiencies, compliance requirements, or selection challenges
- Buyer roles and project constraints where relevant

### 3.3 Requirements and success criteria

- Technical inputs, standards, loads, media, environment, interfaces, dimensions, lifecycle, or service expectations
- Clear separation between common criteria and project-specific confirmation

### 3.4 Solution approach

- Architecture, workflow, selection method, or engineering logic
- Explanation of how the approach addresses each requirement
- Diagram or process visual when it improves understanding
- Boundaries, assumptions, and dependencies

### 3.5 Products and components

- Relevant products and their role in the solution
- Crawlable links to product/category pages
- Compatibility or configuration notes
- No inclusion of unrelated products

### 3.6 Technical value

- Performance, reliability, safety, maintenance, integration, quality, or total-cost considerations
- Claims connected to verified product or process evidence
- Trade-offs and selection conditions where useful

### 3.7 Implementation process

- Discovery, requirement confirmation, selection/design, sampling/testing, production, delivery, installation, or support stages as applicable
- Buyer inputs and supplier responsibilities
- No guaranteed timeline without approval

### 3.8 Evidence

- Relevant case studies, testing, certifications, standards, engineering documents, or process evidence
- Explanation of relevance to this solution
- Confidential information excluded unless approved

### 3.9 FAQ and technical resources

- Project qualification, compatibility, standards, customization, implementation, maintenance, or support questions
- Relevant downloads and technical guides

### 3.10 Project inquiry section

- Clear invitation to discuss the application
- Fields for project requirements, environment, technical parameters, quantities, timeline, and file upload where necessary
- Alternative contact path for complex projects

## 4. Content Requirements

- Define a real buyer problem or application, not a vague industry label
- Explain the solution logic before promoting products
- Use verified technical facts and approved diagrams
- Distinguish general guidance from project-specific engineering advice
- State assumptions, limitations, interfaces, and information required for validation
- Connect each product to a defined function in the solution
- Address technical, operational, procurement, and risk considerations
- Provide evidence appropriate to the target market and industry
- Use language familiar to engineers and purchasing teams without unnecessary jargon
- Link to product details instead of duplicating full specifications

Do not claim guaranteed savings, safety, compliance, performance, or project outcomes without verified conditions and evidence.

## 5. CTA Design

Primary CTA options:

- Discuss Your Application
- Request a Solution Review
- Contact an Engineer

Secondary CTA options:

- View Related Products
- Download Solution Guide
- Review Case Study
- Submit Project Requirements

CTA rules:

- Match the CTA to project complexity
- Ask for decision-critical technical inputs
- Allow drawing or specification upload when useful
- Place CTAs after solution overview, evidence, and final summary
- Preserve solution/application context in the inquiry
- Track consultation clicks, resource downloads, form starts, submissions, and qualified outcomes

## 6. Common Errors

- Renaming a product category page as a solution page
- Vague industry claims with no operating context
- Products listed without explaining their solution role
- No requirements, selection logic, limitations, or implementation process
- Generic benefits unsupported by technical evidence
- Case claims unrelated to the proposed solution
- Diagram that is decorative or technically misleading
- Duplicated content across many industry pages with only the industry name changed
- Solution page competing with product/category pages for the same intent
- CTA limited to “Buy Now” for a consultative project
- Form unable to capture project requirements
- Treating general content as professional engineering approval

## 7. Codex Checklist

### Problem and intent

- [ ] Solution targets a defined industry, process, application, or problem
- [ ] Buyer roles, requirements, and project stage are clear
- [ ] Page intent is distinct from product and category pages

### Solution completeness

- [ ] Challenge, requirements, approach, products, evidence, and implementation are connected
- [ ] Every listed product has a defined role
- [ ] Assumptions, dependencies, and limitations are stated
- [ ] Technical claims are supported and project-specific claims are qualified
- [ ] Diagrams and media are accurate and useful

### Trust and evidence

- [ ] Standards, tests, cases, and capabilities are relevant to the solution
- [ ] Confidential or unsupported customer information is absent
- [ ] Company capability and product certification are distinguished
- [ ] General guidance is separated from engineering validation

### Conversion

- [ ] CTA reflects a consultative B2B sales process
- [ ] Inquiry captures necessary project inputs
- [ ] Related products, cases, resources, and contact paths are linked
- [ ] Solution context and source page are measurable through the inquiry journey

### Technical quality

- [ ] H1, title, canonical, breadcrumb, and content align with solution intent
- [ ] Product and resource links resolve directly to canonical URLs
- [ ] Main content and diagrams are accessible on mobile
- [ ] Structured data, if used, matches visible content
- [ ] Forms, uploads, tracking, and consent behavior are tested
