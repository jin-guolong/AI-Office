# Blog Topic Cluster Rules for Industrial B2B English Websites

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Codex must plan blog topic clusters for overseas English-language websites serving industrial machinery, electrical equipment, and industrial component buyers.

A topic cluster must build useful subject coverage around real products, applications, and buyer decisions. It must support qualified discovery and guide readers toward relevant category, product, solution, case study, resource, or inquiry pages. Publishing volume alone is not a goal.

## 2. Scope

These rules apply to:

- Industrial machinery: production, processing, packaging, material handling, automation, maintenance, and related equipment
- Electrical equipment: power distribution, control, protection, switching, enclosures, motors, drives, and related systems
- Industrial components: mechanical, electrical, pneumatic, hydraulic, sealing, fastening, sensing, connection, and replacement components

Content must target overseas engineers, procurement managers, maintenance teams, project owners, OEMs, system integrators, distributors, and technical decision-makers.

## 3. Required Inputs

Before planning a cluster, confirm:

- Target products and solution priorities
- Target countries and English variant where relevant
- Buyer roles, industries, applications, and purchasing stages
- Approved product data, standards, certifications, test evidence, and terminology
- Existing category, product, solution, case, blog, FAQ, and resource URLs
- GSC queries and landing-page data when available
- Sales, RFQ, support, distributor, and customer-question evidence
- Content production capacity and available subject-matter reviewers

If technical evidence or reviewer access is missing, reduce the topic scope or mark the topic as blocked. Do not fill factual gaps with assumptions.

## 4. Cluster Architecture

Each cluster must contain:

1. One defined pillar topic
2. Supporting subtopics with distinct buyer questions
3. One preferred page for each search intent
4. Links between informational and commercial pages
5. A defined conversion path
6. A measurement plan

Recommended relationship:

`Commercial hub ↔ Pillar guide ↔ Supporting articles/FAQs ↔ Product/Solution/Case/Resource ↔ Inquiry`

The pillar may be a category, solution, application, or guide page. Do not automatically create a blog pillar when a commercial page is the correct primary destination.

## 5. Buyer-Journey Coverage

| Stage | Buyer need | Suitable content |
|---|---|---|
| Discover | Understand a technology, product family, or problem | Definitions, system overviews, application introductions |
| Evaluate | Compare types, methods, materials, or technologies | Comparison and selection guides |
| Validate | Check standards, compatibility, performance, and risk | Technical explainers, test methods, compliance guides |
| Specify | Determine model, size, configuration, or requirements | Sizing, specification, configuration, and checklist content |
| Implement | Install, operate, integrate, or commission | Procedures and implementation guides |
| Maintain | Inspect, troubleshoot, replace, or improve reliability | Maintenance and troubleshooting guides |
| Source | Evaluate suppliers, documentation, and RFQ inputs | Supplier-evaluation and RFQ preparation guides |

A cluster should cover the stages relevant to the product. Do not force every stage when it has no credible buyer need.

## 6. Topic Models by Industry

### 6.1 Industrial machinery

Build topics around:

- Machine selection by material, process, output, tolerance, environment, and automation level
- Capacity, throughput, accuracy, energy, footprint, changeover, and integration factors
- Upstream/downstream equipment and line compatibility
- Installation, commissioning, operation, cleaning, and preventive maintenance
- Failure modes, diagnostic steps, wear parts, and service intervals
- Safety, guarding, applicable standards, and documentation
- Total cost, spare parts, training, support, and RFQ requirements

Never publish operating or safety instructions without approved technical review.

### 6.2 Electrical equipment

Build topics around:

- Voltage, current, power, frequency, phase, duty, breaking capacity, and protection requirements
- Coordination, selectivity, derating, enclosure, ingress protection, temperature, altitude, and environment
- Applicable IEC, UL, NEC, regional, or product-specific standards
- System compatibility, wiring, communication, control, and integration
- Selection, installation, testing, inspection, and maintenance considerations
- Failure symptoms and safe diagnostic boundaries
- Documentation, certification, project specification, and sourcing requirements

Do not provide hazardous live-work instructions or claim compliance from component data alone.

### 6.3 Industrial components

Build topics around:

- Material, dimension, tolerance, load, speed, pressure, temperature, medium, and environment
- Fit, interface, interchangeability, compatibility, and replacement selection
- Wear, corrosion, fatigue, leakage, vibration, contamination, and lifecycle factors
- Installation, lubrication, inspection, storage, and maintenance
- Custom versus standard components and manufacturability
- Drawings, tolerances, testing, quality control, packaging, and traceability
- OEM, distributor, bulk sourcing, and RFQ information

Do not claim interchangeability or compatibility without verified dimensional and performance evidence.

## 7. Topic Selection Workflow

For every proposed topic:

1. Identify the buyer role and target market.
2. Define one primary question, search intent, and buyer stage.
3. Identify the relevant product, solution, or commercial page.
4. Review existing internal content for overlap.
5. Use GSC, sales questions, support records, product taxonomy, and SERP patterns as evidence.
6. Confirm that source material and technical review are available.
7. Define the unique buyer value the article will add.
8. Specify required sections, evidence, internal links, and CTA.
9. Assign one canonical target URL.
10. Set priority and measurement criteria.

Reject topics that are only broad traffic opportunities, unrelated trends, rewritten competitor articles, or questions the company cannot answer accurately.

## 8. Topic Brief Standard

Every approved topic brief must contain:

| Field | Required output |
|---|---|
| Working title | Clear buyer-focused title |
| Target market | Country/region and English variant |
| Audience | Buyer role and technical level |
| Primary intent | One defined intent |
| Buyer stage | One primary stage |
| Business connection | Product, solution, or service supported |
| Preferred URL | Existing or proposed canonical URL |
| Core question | The decision or task to resolve |
| Required sections | Logical answer structure |
| Technical sources | Approved documents and reviewers |
| Evidence | Data, standards, tests, examples, or diagrams needed |
| Internal links | Pillar, commercial, supporting, and next-step pages |
| CTA | Stage-appropriate buyer action |
| Differentiation | Original value beyond existing content |
| KPI | Query, engagement, assisted conversion, or lead signal |

## 9. Cannibalization and Page-Boundary Rules

- One primary intent must map to one preferred page
- Merge close variants that require the same answer
- Separate a topic only when the buyer task, product entity, application, standard, or technical depth is materially different
- A blog must not replace a category or product page for direct commercial intent
- A blog may support a solution page but must not duplicate its complete commercial proposition
- FAQs should answer narrow questions and link to the full guide when deeper explanation is needed
- Update a strong existing page before creating a near-duplicate

Flag overlapping titles, H1s, GSC query sets, internal anchors, and content outlines before approving a new URL.

## 10. Internal Linking Rules

Each article must normally link to:

- Its pillar or topic hub
- The most relevant product, category, solution, or application page
- One or more supporting articles when useful
- A relevant case, FAQ, datasheet, manual, or technical resource when available
- A buyer-appropriate conversion path

Use descriptive, natural anchors. Do not force exact-match anchors or add unrelated product links. All destinations must be canonical, accessible, and current.

## 11. Publication Sequence

Prioritize in this order unless data supports another sequence:

1. Fix or establish the commercial hub
2. Publish the pillar or primary selection resource
3. Publish high-value validation and specification topics
4. Add implementation and maintenance support
5. Add narrow FAQs and long-tail supporting content
6. Consolidate, update, or retire weak overlaps

Do not publish a cluster of informational pages with no complete commercial destination.

## 12. Measurement and Review

Track by cluster and page type:

- GSC impressions, clicks, CTR, query coverage, country, and landing page
- GA4 organic landing sessions, engagement, downloads, product/solution progression, and key events
- Assisted RFQs or qualified inquiries where attribution is available
- Internal-link usage and conversion-path progression
- Content decay, outdated specifications, broken links, and query/page drift

Review new or materially updated content after sufficient crawling and data collection. Do not declare success from rankings or traffic alone.

## 13. Codex Checklist

- [ ] Cluster is tied to a priority product, solution, application, or buyer problem
- [ ] Target market, audience, and English variant are defined
- [ ] One pillar/hub and one preferred page per intent are assigned
- [ ] Topics cover real buyer decisions across relevant stages
- [ ] Machinery, electrical, or component-specific technical factors are included
- [ ] Existing content and cannibalization have been checked
- [ ] Approved sources and technical reviewers are identified
- [ ] Every topic has distinct value, required evidence, and a business connection
- [ ] Internal links connect informational and commercial pages
- [ ] CTA matches the buyer stage
- [ ] Publication sequence begins with a complete commercial destination
- [ ] Measurement includes qualified buyer progression, not traffic alone
