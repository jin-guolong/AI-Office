# Industrial B2B Client Operations PPT Report Template

## 1. Purpose

This template defines the slide structure for monthly client operations reports covering SEO, website optimization, content, technical implementation, analytics, and next-month priorities.

The deck must support a client meeting and management decision. Each slide should communicate one main conclusion, show enough evidence to support it, and state the relevant business implication or action.

## 2. Report Setup

Before building the deck, confirm:

- Client and website
- Reporting period and comparison period
- Target countries, languages, products, and solutions
- Business objectives and primary conversions
- GA4, GSC, CRM, crawl, project, and content data availability
- Client audience: owner, marketing, sales, engineering, or management
- Meeting duration and required decisions
- Brand template, aspect ratio, fonts, colors, and confidentiality requirements

Use a 16:9 layout unless the client template requires another format.

## 3. Recommended Core Deck

### Slide 1 — Cover

Include:

- Client name and website
- Report title
- Reporting period
- Prepared by and presentation date
- Confidentiality label when required

Primary message: identify the report clearly.

### Slide 2 — Executive Summary

Include:

- Overall status
- Three most important outcomes
- One main risk/blocker
- One main next-month priority

Primary message example: “Qualified non-brand visibility improved in priority product categories, while inquiry measurement still requires correction.”

### Slide 3 — KPI Scorecard

Show five to eight decision-relevant KPIs:

- Organic clicks or landing sessions
- Qualified target-market traffic
- Priority non-brand visibility
- Organic key events or leads
- Qualified inquiries when available
- Conversion rate
- Priority indexed pages or other health indicator

Include current value, comparison, change, target/baseline, and status.

### Slide 4 — Organic Search Performance

Show the trend and explain:

- What changed
- When it changed
- Which market, device, query group, or page type caused it
- Whether the change is meaningful

Use one primary time-series chart and limited annotations.

### Slide 5 — Search Demand and Query Insights

Show:

- Priority query/topic clusters
- Brand versus non-brand mix
- Emerging buyer questions or product demand
- High-impression/low-CTR or wrong-page opportunities

Primary message must describe buyer demand, not merely keyword movement.

### Slide 6 — Landing Page and Page-Type Performance

Compare relevant page groups:

- Homepage/company
- Product categories
- Product details
- Solutions/applications
- Case studies
- Blog/FAQ/resources

Show which page type is contributing to discovery, evaluation, and conversion.

### Slide 7 — Conversion and Buyer Journey

Visualize:

`Organic landing → Commercial/technical page → CTA/form start → Submission → Qualified inquiry`

Identify the strongest path, biggest drop-off, data gap, and recommended correction.

### Slide 8 — Work Completed

Group completed work by:

- Page/content optimization
- Technical/HTML work
- Internal linking/schema
- Data/tracking
- Research/planning

For each group, state scope, business reason, validation, and status. Do not count drafts awaiting approval as completed implementation.

### Slide 9 — Technical and Website Health

Show only material issues:

- Crawling/indexation
- Canonical/redirects
- Broken or orphan pages
- Mobile/performance
- Structured data
- Analytics/form tracking

Use status, affected scope, risk, and action. Avoid raw audit exports.

### Slide 10 — Content and Opportunity Pipeline

Show:

- Product/category/solution gaps
- Topic clusters and buyer stages
- Pages being updated, created, consolidated, or reviewed
- Technical evidence or client inputs required

Connect every proposed content item to a business page and buyer need.

### Slide 11 — Risks, Blockers, and Client Decisions

For each item include:

- Issue or decision
- Business impact
- Required owner
- Due date
- Consequence of delay

Make client requests explicit and manageable.

### Slide 12 — Next-Month Action Plan

Show three to seven committed actions with:

- Priority
- Deliverable/scope
- Business objective
- Owner and dependency
- Success measure
- Target date

Separate committed actions from optional backlog items.

### Slide 13 — Closing and Discussion

Include:

- Main conclusion
- Decisions required today
- Immediate next step
- Contact information

## 4. Optional Slides

Add only when they support the meeting:

- Country or regional performance
- Device performance
- Product-family deep dive
- Solution-page performance
- Competitor/SERP observations
- Case study or campaign deep dive
- Core Web Vitals/template analysis
- Conversion tracking diagnosis
- Content calendar
- Methodology and metric definitions
- Detailed URL/query appendix

Keep technical detail in an appendix when management does not need it for the primary decision.

## 5. Slide Content Standard

Every analytical slide must include:

1. A conclusion-led title
2. Reporting/comparison period
3. Data source
4. Clearly labeled visualization or table
5. One to three supporting observations
6. Business implication
7. Recommended action when applicable

Avoid titles such as “GSC Data” or “Traffic Overview.” Use titles that state the finding.

## 6. Chart and Table Rules

- Choose the chart based on the question, not decoration.
- Use line charts for time, bars for category comparison, and funnels for buyer-stage progression.
- Avoid pie charts with many categories and avoid 3D charts.
- Keep axes, units, periods, filters, and comparison basis visible.
- Use consistent colors for current period, comparison, positive, warning, and risk states.
- Do not use green/red alone to communicate meaning.
- Start bar charts at zero unless a justified exception is clearly marked.
- Do not combine metrics with incompatible scales without clear treatment.
- Limit tables to decision-relevant rows; move full data to appendix.
- Show absolute values with percentages when percentage changes may mislead.

## 7. Data Interpretation Rules

- GSC clicks and GA4 sessions are different metrics and need not match.
- Average position is an aggregate diagnostic metric, not a ranking promise.
- State whether leads are raw submissions or qualified inquiries.
- Separate correlation, hypothesis, and confirmed cause.
- Flag low volume, thresholding, consent loss, tracking changes, seasonality, and incomplete periods.
- Do not attribute revenue to SEO without a valid attribution and CRM basis.
- Use forecasts only as labeled scenarios with assumptions.

## 8. Client-Facing Language

- Use professional, direct English unless another language is requested.
- Translate technical findings into buyer or business impact.
- Define acronyms on first use when the audience may not know them.
- Avoid ranking guarantees, exaggerated claims, and unexplained SEO jargon.
- Use neutral language for delays and blockers; state evidence, owner, and action.
- Keep sentences short enough for slides; place detailed explanation in speaker notes or appendix.

## 9. Design and Accessibility Rules

- Use the approved client brand consistently.
- Maintain clear hierarchy, alignment, whitespace, and contrast.
- Use readable type sizes for presentation conditions.
- Keep important labels and annotations legible without zooming.
- Use high-quality relevant industrial visuals with permission.
- Do not use decorative stock imagery as evidence of client capability.
- Add alternative text when the final delivery format and workflow support it.
- Ensure exported PDF remains readable and links work when applicable.

## 10. Speaker Notes Standard

For each core slide, notes may include:

- What the presenter should emphasize
- Data caveats
- Likely client question and answer
- Decision required
- Backup evidence location

Do not place confidential internal speculation in notes delivered to the client.

## 11. Production Workflow

1. Validate source data, periods, filters, and metric definitions.
2. Draft the executive narrative before designing slides.
3. Select only evidence that supports client decisions.
4. Build the core deck, then add necessary appendix slides.
5. Recalculate and cross-check every displayed number.
6. Apply client brand and chart standards.
7. Render all slides and visually inspect them.
8. Check overflow, clipping, alignment, contrast, chart labels, dates, and links.
9. Verify consistency between deck, source workbook, and written SEO report.
10. Save editable source and final client version in the approved report directory.

## 12. File and Delivery Standard

Recommended naming:

`client-seo-operations-report-YYYY-MM-final.pptx`

Store final client reports in:

`/05_Report/[client-or-project]/YYYY/MM/`

Deliver with:

- Editable PPTX
- PDF when requested
- Source data workbook or appendix when included in scope
- File name and location
- Reporting period and data sources
- Summary of changes from prior version
- Known limitations and pending client inputs

Do not silently overwrite an approved report. Preserve draft, review, and final versions when needed.

## 13. Codex Final Checklist

- [ ] Client, website, period, comparison, market, objective, and audience are confirmed
- [ ] Data sources, filters, timezone, conversions, and limitations are documented
- [ ] Executive summary states outcomes, risk, and next priority
- [ ] Each slide has one conclusion-led message
- [ ] KPIs connect search visibility to buyer action and qualified inquiries
- [ ] GSC, GA4, CRM, and crawl metrics are labeled correctly
- [ ] Charts use correct scales, units, dates, and comparisons
- [ ] Completed work is separated from pending or blocked work
- [ ] Risks and client decisions include owner, impact, and due date
- [ ] Next-month plan is prioritized, measurable, and realistic
- [ ] Claims, causes, and forecasts are properly qualified
- [ ] Client-facing language is professional and decision-oriented
- [ ] All slides were rendered and visually inspected
- [ ] No overflow, clipping, unreadable labels, broken links, or inconsistent numbers remain
- [ ] Editable and final files are stored and named correctly
- [ ] Confidential information and internal notes are protected
